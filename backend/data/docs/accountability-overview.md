# English Learner Accountability Overview

## Why it matters
Under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA), states include **progress toward English language proficiency** in their school accountability systems. Schools are also held accountable for the academic achievement of English learner subgroups.

## What is measured
- **English language proficiency progress**: based on student growth on ACCESS for ELLs from one year to the next, measured against a student-specific target.
- **Academic achievement**: English language arts and mathematics assessment results for the EL subgroup, including former ELs for up to four years in some reporting.
- **Participation**: schools must test at least 95% of eligible students, including ELs.

## What teachers can do
- Make sure every EL is tested during the ACCESS window.
- Keep ELD plan and accommodation records current so the data is accurate.
- Use domain scores from the previous year to set instructional goals.

## Data accuracy
Student EL status, entry date in a US school, and program placement are entered in the student information system. Errors can affect accountability results. Report errors to the school registrar and the ESOL coordinator.
