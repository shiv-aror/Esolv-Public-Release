# ELD Instructional & Testing Accommodations Guide

## Purpose
English learners (ELs) receive accommodations so they can show what they know while they are still acquiring English. Accommodations change *how* a student accesses content or shows learning. They do not change the expectations for what is learned.

## Accommodation categories
Accommodations are grouped into four categories:

- **Presentation**: how information is delivered. Examples: bilingual glossaries, read-aloud of directions, visuals and graphic organizers, simplified language in directions, translated directions.
- **Response**: how the student responds. Examples: oral responses, drawing or labeling, sentence frames and word banks, use of a bilingual dictionary, responding in the home language where permitted.
- **Setting**: where the work happens. Examples: small-group setting, preferential seating near the teacher, a quiet space for testing.
- **Timing and scheduling**: when and how long. Examples: extended time, frequent breaks, testing across multiple sessions.

## Accommodations by English proficiency level
- **Levels 1-2 (Entering / Emerging)**: bilingual glossaries, visuals, sentence frames, oral responses, extended time, and translated directions are most commonly used.
- **Levels 3-4 (Developing / Expanding)**: word banks, bilingual dictionaries, graphic organizers, and extended time. Sentence frames are phased out gradually.
- **Levels 5-6 (Bridging / Reaching)**: usually limited to extended time and occasional dictionary use. Review whether each accommodation is still needed.

## Documenting accommodations
1. Record each accommodation in the student's ELD plan with the date it was selected and who selected it.
2. Accommodations used on state assessments must also be used in regular classroom instruction and classroom tests.
3. Review the list at least once a year and whenever the student's English proficiency level changes.
4. A student's IEP or 504 plan accommodations are documented separately and apply in addition to ELD accommodations. Do not replace one with the other.

## Who decides
The ESOL teacher recommends accommodations with input from the classroom teachers, the student, and the family. Confirm with the school ESOL coordinator or ELD lead if a needed accommodation is not listed.
